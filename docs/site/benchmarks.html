Current defaults (2026-08-19)

Frontier-parallel bitset determinization and antichain evaluation of closed sentences are now Peanut's defaults. Measured on a quiet 18-core Apple Silicon machine, one engine process at a time, 6 GB budget on both engines, msd, bench/defaults_bench.py:

Where Walnut is still faster: tail-c. Walnut's CCLS strategy answers it in 10.6 s; Peanut's best on that row is 16.10 s via learnfe. That is the only case in this table Peanut does not win, and the margin is 1.5x. Everywhere else, Peanut now beats the best Walnut strategy for that case: 4.6x on tail-a, 8.4x on tail-b, 33x on prism-1, 540x on single6, 178x on Tribonacci FE by the ladder and 756x by the learner. State counts agree with Walnut's to the dead state on every row where both finish, which is the correctness cross-check behind all of this.

The Nicol correction

An earlier version of this benchmark compared Peanut only against Walnut's default determinization strategy (plain subset construction), although Walnut 7+ ships Brzozowski and on-the-fly strategies of its own. John Nicol, Walnut's developer, pointed this out, and he was right. The claim that "Walnut OOMs where Peanut answers" in that first benchmark was an artefact of running the default strategy only, and is withdrawn. With the right strategy chosen per query, Walnut 8-dev answers every case in this table too.

What remains true after the correction: no single Walnut strategy covers the whole panel, so a run needs per-query tuning that Peanut's one default does not; state counts agree everywhere both tools finish, an independent cross-check on both implementations; and learnfe, Peanut's reimplementation of Mazen Khodier's self-verifying predicates, is the largest single gap on Tribonacci FE, a gap that closes as Walnut 8 adopts the same construction. The difference this benchmark measures is mostly one of defaults, not of algorithms, and Walnut had the underlying algorithms first. Thanks to John Nicol for the correction.
