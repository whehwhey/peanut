## 2. The proof method — one real gap, and it is repairable

### 2.1 The induction is otherwise sound, and stronger than the document claims

I re-derived §4's argument line by line. Write `A*(m) := κm + lo + T[m]` (the encoded
`A_{m+1}`), and for each `m` let `x = x(m)`, `g = g(m)` be given by `B1`.

* `Σⱼ aⱼ xⱼ = τx + (A₁−τ) + Σ_{j>g} aⱼ = τx + c_g` — **checked by hand**, so the encoded
  `A*(m)` really is the `a`-weighted sum of the encoded block.
* `0 ≤ Σ_{j>g} aⱼ ≤ τ − a₁ < τ` and strictly decreasing in `g`, so the `c_g` are pairwise
  distinct mod `τ`. **Verified for all 249 forms** (assertion in `ref_build.py`), so
  `(x,g)` is indeed recovered from `A*(m)` with no existential.
* Induction step, with `S_m = ⋃_{j<m}({A(j)} ∪ blk(j))` and the IH `encoded = true` below `m`:
  (i) P9 ⇒ `[1,x) ⊆ S_m`; (ii) P8 ⇒ `x` is not an `A`-value, **P5 ⇒ `x` exceeds every
  earlier `B`-value**, so `x = mex(S_m)`; (iii) the other block elements are `B`-values
  `> last(m−1)`, hence `∉ S_m`; (iv) P10_g puts the skipped `x+g` into `S_m`. Hence the
  encoded block is the true block and `A*(m) = A(m)`.
* Two things the document under-sells: the obligations do **not** assume Fokkink–Joshi's
  "at most one skip per block" (an object violating it makes P8 or P9 FALSE, as the
  negative controls show), and they do **not** need "the uniqueness half of Lemma 3" —
  P8 + P7 + P5 + P9 prove complementarity and monotonicity outright, so the encoded pair
  is pinned to the mex generator term by term. The appeal to Lemma 3 is correct but
  redundant.
* Final step re-checked: `Aₙ − κn = (lo−κ) + T[n−1]`, a constant plus a one-place shift of
  a `τ`-automatic sequence, hence `τ`-automatic. Correct.

### 2.2 The gap: `LAST` is inverted, so P5 and P6 are vacuous

`ATTACK-3.md` §3 and `explore/attack3_general.py` both define

        LAST(m,y) :≡ ⋁_{g<k} G_g(m, y+k) ∨ G_k(m, y+k−1)

`$G_g(m, y+k)` substitutes `x := y+k`, i.e. it asserts **`y = x − k`**, not `y = x + k`.
`LAST` is therefore the block start *minus* `k`, not the block's last element. Engine
witness, `a=(1,4)` (block 0 is `{1,2}`, last element `2`):

        WITNESS y=0 states=2 len=0 ms=0 :: $LAST(0,y)
        WITNESS y=6 ... :: $LAST(3,y)      WITNESS x=7 ... :: $B1(3,x)

so `LAST(m) = x(m) − 1` here. Consequences:

* **P5 as run** says `x(m) − k < x(m+1)` (resp. `x(m)−k+1 < x(m+1)`). By hand: P7 gives
  `A*(m+1) > A*(m)`, i.e. `τ(x(m+1)−x(m)) > c_g − c_{g'} > −τ`, so `x(m+1) ≥ x(m)`; P5-as-run
  is an immediate consequence of P7 and carries **no** information beyond it.
* **P6 as run** says `x(m) − k < A*(m) = τx(m)+c_g` — a tautology for `τ ≥ 2`, `c_g ≥ 0`.
* Step (ii)/(iii) of §4's induction cite P5 for "`x` is strictly above every earlier
  `B`-value". With P5 vacuous that step is unsupported, and the induction stalls in the
  case `x(m) ≤ max blk(m−1)` (which nothing else in the list excludes: P8 only forbids
  `x` from being an `A`-value, P9 only constrains `[1,x)`). **As shipped, the eleven
  obligations do not prove what §4 says they prove.**
* Empirical confirmation that P5-as-run is dead weight: over 560 single-edit perturbations
  of two *verified* DFAOs (§4.4 below), the corrected P5 fires **181** times; the shipped
  P5 fires **0** times.
* Note `explore/attack3_verify.py`, the older `k=2`-only encoding, has the **correct**
  predicate (`B2(m,y) :≡ E x. BB(m,x,y)`), so the bug was introduced in the general-`k`
  rewrite. The document's claim that the two encodings "agree" is true of their verdicts
  but hides that one of them is strictly stronger.

**The fix** is one line — `LAST(m,y) :≡ ⋁_{g<k} (∃x. G_g(m,x) ∧ y = x+k) ∨ (∃x. G_k(m,x) ∧ y = x+k−1)`.

scripts with **my** builder and the corrected `LAST`, and re-runs them:

the proof that holds.

### 2.4 Three obligations are tautologies of the encoding

Re-derived by hand and confirmed on 107 well-formed **random** DFAOs (13 of 120 were
rejected by `def` itself) and on all 560 perturbations:

| obligation | fires on 560 perturbations | fires on 107 random DFAOs | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1a, P1b, P2u | 0 | 0 | tautologies (`AA` is a function by construction; `x` unique because the `c_g` are distinct mod `τ`) |
| P6, P7 | 0 | 0 | free: `A*(m+1)−A*(m) ≥ κ−(hi−lo) ≥ (k−2)τ+3 > 0` |
| P4 | 12 | 88 | content, but its second conjunct `AA(0,A₁)` is *implied* by `G_k(0,1)` (`τ·1 + c_k = A₁`) |
| P5 (corrected) | 181 | 93 | **load-bearing for the induction** |
| P2, P8, P10_g | 372 / 190 / 202 | 107 / 104 / 107 | content |
| P9 | **560** | 107 | the workhorse — catches every perturbation |

So the effective obligation set is `{P2, P4, P5, P8, P9, P10_g}`; the document presents
eleven and five of them are free. That is a presentation defect, not an error.

## 5. The negative controls — reproduced, and sharper than claimed

I re-derived the near-miss automata myself (guessing from short prefixes) and measured
their true breaking point against my C generator, then ran **my** obligations on them.

| form | states | first index where the guess ≠ the mex sequence | obligations |
|---|---|---|---|
| `(1,6)` | 15 | **335 132** | P8 FALSE, P9 FALSE |
| `(2,9)` | 26 | **13 535 104** | P8 FALSE, P9 FALSE |
| `(1,12)` | 35 | **51 280 580** | P8 FALSE, P9 FALSE |

* The `(2,9)` witness reproduces **exactly**: `witness $B1(m,x) & t>=1 & t<x & ~(E j. j<m
  & $USED(j,t))` returns `m=13535104  t=28300672  x=28300673` in **235 ms** (the document
  says 224 ms) — and that `m` is precisely the first mismatch index my generator finds
  independently. The equivalence-oracle claim of §5 is real.
* The `(1,12)` 35-state near-miss is **better** than the document says: it is correct on
  `5.128·10⁷` terms, not `2.5·10⁷`. P8 and P9 both return FALSE (P2 and P10 blew the RSS
  watchdog at ~6.8 GB and were not needed). The moral — prefix agreement is not evidence
  — is if anything understated.
* **One claim is wrong.** §5 says these guesses "passed a long prefix check [and] were
  nevertheless caught by the engine", naming `a=(1,6)`/15 states first. The `(1,6)`
  15-state guess breaks at index **335 132**, *inside* the sweep's own `Ncheck = 400 000`
  prefix check, so the adaptive ladder rejects it before the engine ever sees it. It is a
  valid demonstration that prefix agreement at 3·10⁵ is worthless; it is not an example of
  the engine catching what the prefix check missed. The genuine examples are `(2,9)` and
  `(1,12)`.

## 6. The tables and the structural observations

Recomputed from the stored records with my own code. **Every row of both §6 tables
matches exactly** — the rusty table (`d`, `τ`, `κ`, `A₁`, `A₁`-bounded, states, alphabet,
window, `2τ−1`) and the 29-row (`k,τ`) table (forms, `A₁`-bounded, open, state min–max,
max alphabet). 249 forms, 61 non-`A₁`-bounded, and the 61-element list is the same set.

* Obs. 1: `max((hi−lo+1) − (2τ−1)) = −1`, `min(lo − (A₁−τ)) = −2`, `max(hi − (A₁+τ−1)) = 0`.
  Exact (these come from the DFAO's output alphabet, which is exact, not from a prefix).
* Obs. 2: rusty state counts `2,3,6,9,11,16,19,21,26,29,31,36`; successive `|Q|(d+3)−|Q|(d)`
  = `7,8,10,10,10,10,10,10,10`, so `+10` for `d = 3..9`, seven instances, failing exactly
  at the two `A₁`-bounded members. Predictions `|Q|(13,14,15) = 39,41,46` are the
  law's own extrapolation. Confirmed as stated.
* Obs. 3: 17 forms with `|Q| > 2τ−1`, 30 forms with `hi > κ`, max overflow 6, first at
  `(1,4)`. Numbers confirmed. **The wording is wrong**: Lemma 11/Lemma 5 and Theorem
  12/Theorem 4 are both stated *only for `A₁`-bounded* forms, and I checked that **every
  `A₁`-bounded form in the sweep satisfies all of them** — `hi ≤ κ`, `lo ≥ A₁−τ+1`,
  `hi ≤ A₁+τ−1`, `|Q| ≤ 2τ−1`. Nothing published is contradicted; the correct statement
  is that the *conclusions* of Lemma 5 and Theorem 4 fail outside their hypothesis, which
  is what makes the general case hard. "Lemma 11 fails" and "the state bound … does not
  survive" should be rephrased.

## 7. What is actually new

* **New:** machine proofs of Conjecture 4 for **60** forms no published theorem reaches —
  the 61 non-`A₁`-bounded forms of the sweep minus `a=(1,3)`. Headline: the rusty numbers
  `a=(1,d)` for **`d = 4..12`**, the case *Integers* 26 #A24 §5 names as a challenge. The
  document's ledger says exactly this and is honest about `(1,3)`: the paper exhibits a
  4-DFAO for it (Fig. 7) and asserts the Walnut verification applies, without transcript
  or state count. My independent check confirms the minimal automaton has 6 states.
* **Not new:** the 188 `A₁`-bounded forms (Theorem 4/12), `(1,1)` (Zaslavsky),
  `(1,1,1)`/`(1,1,1,1)` (Bosma et al.), `(1,2)`/`(2,1)` (Theorems 5,6 / 1,2).
  Anti-`k`-bonacci minimal state counts are `k` for `k = 2..7`, matching Theorem 3/8.
* **Still open, and the document says so:** Conjecture 4 in general. Nothing here is
  uniform in `a`; 249 instances is 249 instances. Obs. 3 is the right diagnosis of why —
  a general proof cannot just relax Theorem 4's constant, because the substitution
  alphabet is no longer "value of `Aₙ mod κ`".

## 8. Residual caveats

* Everything rests on the engine being a correct decision procedure for `⟨ℕ,+,V_τ⟩` plus
  the DFAO. That is not formally verified. The evidence gathered here — 560 perturbations
  and 107 random automata all rejected, three near-misses rejected at the exact index an
  independent generator finds, compiled predicates matching ground truth tuple for tuple,
  and a second encoding agreeing on 34 records — is strong but is not a proof of the tool.
* `a = (1,12)` needs ~2.9 GB; `k=2, τ ≥ 14` and the larger `k=3,4` cases are untried, so
  the "249" is a budget boundary, not a mathematical one.
