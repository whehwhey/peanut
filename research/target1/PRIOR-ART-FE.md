# Prior art: size of the minimal equality-of-factors automaton vs DFAO size m

Question asked: is anything already known, published, bounding |FE| (minimal automaton
for `FE(i,j,l) := A t. t<l => T[i+t]=T[j+t]`) as a function of m, the number of states
of the DFAO producing T? And is our empirical claim ("typical minimal |FE| is
polynomial, ~m^3-m^4, no exponential family found") new, known, or refuted?

## What is known

**1. No published bound, upper or lower, ties minimal |FE| to m specifically, before
Khodier's 2026 thesis.** CRS 2012 and Schaeffer's 2013 thesis both state only the
*generic, formula-agnostic* worst case for the standard automata-logic decision
procedure: quantifier elimination by determinize-then-project can blow up
exponentially per alternation, so an arbitrary formula with q states involved has a
**tower-of-exponentials** upper bound whose height is the number of quantifier
alternations (CRS 2012, Theorem 42 / "Closing remarks": for x,y generated by automata
with <=q states and differing factor sets, a distinguishing factor of length bounded by
a tower of 2's in q exists; Schaeffer 2013, Section 3.2.3: "our upper bound is (roughly)
a tower of exponentials... in practice we have never encountered performance close to
the predicted upper bound"). Neither paper isolates EqFac/FE as a special case with its
own tighter bound, and neither gives a lower-bound example.

**2. Khodier's thesis (2026) is the first to state a formula-specific upper bound for
FE**, and it is *single* exponential, not a tower: for the two-free-position
reformulation `A u,v. (u>=i & u<i+n & u+j=v+i) => T[u]=T[v]` (which needs only one
block of universal quantification rather than nested alternations), automata in base k
are bounded by **2^(9m^2) states**, m = DFAO size (thesis Ch.8, p.47-48, and the
Tribonacci worked example: final FE has 26 states, m for Tribonacci's DFAO is small,
but the *intermediate* automaton built by the naive one-shot Walnut formulation peaked
at 323,831,403 states / 300 GB — reformulating to the u,v version above cut the
intermediate peak to 18,853 states, still far above the final 26).

**3. Khodier's Theorem 5 (self-verifying predicates, Ch.3 / arXiv:2507.19717) gives a
construction whose *running time* is polynomial in N (the size of the minimal FE
automaton) and m — but this does not bound N itself.** It sidesteps the intermediate
blowup by using Angluin's L* with membership/hypothesis queries answered by
polynomial-size auxiliary automata (each membership query needs only O(log max(i,j,n))
states via BFS reachability, not full determinization), so construction cost tracks the
*final* minimal size rather than any intermediate blowup. Aspect (B) of Khodier's Open
Problem 1 (efficient construction) is exactly what this theorem addresses, and it is a
genuine result. It says nothing about whether N is ever superpolynomial in m — that is
explicitly left open.

**4. Open Problem 1 itself (Khodier thesis, Ch.8, verbatim) is unresolved as of the
thesis date (Jan 2026):**

> "The first [aspect] concerns the size of the minimal automaton for the equality of
> factors predicate relative to the size of the morphism. We believe that this
> relationship is exponential, but currently, no class of examples is known that proves
> this behavior."

This is a direct, dated statement from the primary source (Khodier is Shallit's
student; the thesis is the most recent and most specific treatment of this exact
question in the literature) that **no exponential-blowup family is known to anyone**,
not just to us. The 2^(9m^2) figure is an *upper bound* from one particular
formulation, explicitly not claimed to be tight, and not accompanied by any matching
lower bound.

**5. No hits for a bespoke "polynomial-size automaton for equality of factors" theorem**
anywhere in CRS 2012, Goč-Schaeffer-Shallit 2012/2013 (which bounds subword-complexity
*function growth*, O(n), not automaton *state counts*, and is not about FE sizing at
all), Bell-Charlier-Fraenkel-Rigo (on ultimately-periodic-set decision problems in
non-standard numeration systems — unrelated to FE sizing), or Shallit's 2023 book
(which uses "FACTOREQ" as a worked Walnut example, per its index, with no stated
general size theorem in terms of m).

**6. Digit-sum-mod-p factor complexity:** searches turned up Coquet 1983 and later
digit-sum-parity work, all about *counting/enumeration* results (e.g. Newman-sum type
identities) for `(-1)^{s_2(n)}` (Thue-Morse) and multiples of 3, not about the minimal
automaton size of FE for `[s_2(n) = a mod p]` or its factor complexity as a function of
p. No prior source was found addressing this specific family's FE size or growth rate;
our `s_2(n) mod p` sweep (TARGET1.md: quadratic in lsd, ~p^2.5 in msd, and the coding
lossiness effect — binary `[s2 != 0 mod p]` inflating |FE| by ~100x over the full
p-letter output at the same DFAO size) appears to be original data.

**7. Tangential general theory not specific to this question:** the classical
non-elementary (tower-of-exponentials) worst case for automata-based decision
procedures over Presburger-style / Büchi arithmetic is a known general phenomenon
(Meyer/Stockmeyer-style results for related logics; Klaedtke and others for
WS1S/Büchi automata complexity) but none of these give a construction that is specific
to, or realized by, the FE predicate — they are generic worst-case complexity results
about the decision procedure, not about this predicate's minimal automaton size. Not
pursued further as it is off the specific question asked.

## Verdict on our claim

Our empirical claim in `docs/TARGET1.md` — "for random morphisms the typical minimal
FE is polynomial, ~2.5-4 m^3 medians across m=2..7, k=2,3; no family showing exponential
growth found; construction-time blowup is real and decoupled from final minimal size" —
is:

- **(a) New, as an empirical/statistical result.** No prior source (including
  Khodier's own thesis, the most specific treatment) reports systematic measurements of
  minimal |FE| across a swept family as a function of m. The thesis states the open
  problem and one upper bound (2^(9m^2)) plus one hand-picked example (Tribonacci,
  26 states); it does not sweep random morphisms or report medians/quartiles.
- **(b) Not already known.** Nobody has published a class of examples with exponential
  minimal |FE| — Khodier's thesis explicitly says so, dated Jan 2026, and nothing found
  in CRS 2012, Schaeffer 2013, Goč-Schaeffer-Shallit, or Shallit's book contradicts or
  supersedes that statement. Our sweep not finding an exponential family in the bulk is
  therefore consistent with, not a refutation of, the field's current position — it's
  additional negative evidence for the same open question, gathered by simulation
  rather than proof.
- **(c) Not refuted.** No published lower-bound family exists to refute it, and no
  published upper bound smaller than 2^(9m^2) forces polynomiality either. The 5.6%
  censored (budget-failed) tail at m>=5 in our sweep is exactly where a hypothetical
  exponential family would have to live and remains unresolved by us or anyone else.

**Important scope caveat, stated plainly:** our result is about the *typical* case
under a specific random-morphism sampler (PRISM), not a proof about *all* automatic
sequences, and Khodier's open problem is about whether *any* class of examples with
exponential blowup exists — a single adversarial family would settle it regardless of
what "typical" looks like. So "no sign of exponential growth in the bulk" is evidence,
not a resolution, and is explicitly flagged as such already in TARGET1.md.
