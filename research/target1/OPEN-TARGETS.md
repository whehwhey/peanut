# OPEN-TARGETS — scouted open problems on k-automatic sequences (uniform morphisms + coding)

Scope: only claims expressible in FO over `<N,+,V_k>` (standard base-k automaticity,
Cobham-style, uniform morphism + coding) — i.e., things `learnfe`/`let`/`witness`/`enum`
attack natively. Excluded on purpose: Fibonacci/Tribonacci/Ostrowski/Pell/metallic
numeration systems, additive-square and block-sum questions (all outside the fragment
even when the source paper mixes them in). Checked against web/arXiv on 2026-08-17;
nothing below is reported settled since its source's last version.

Ranked by (fit to fragment) x (plausibly stuck on compute/bookkeeping) x (source is
explicit that it's open and recent).

---

## 1. Exact state-complexity formula for shifted Thue-Morse, `(t(i+c))` — Open Problem 2 of Moradi-Rampersad-Shallit 2025

**Source.** D. Moradi, N. Rampersad, J. Shallit, *Complexity of Linear Subsequences of
k-Automatic Sequences*, arXiv:2512.10017 (v5, 2 Apr 2026), §4.2, "Open Problem 2".

**Statement (verbatim).** "What is a good formula for the exact number of states in the
minimal automaton generating `(t(i+c))_{i>=0}` with msd-first input, as a function of `c`?"
— `t` the Thue-Morse sequence. They cite a lower bound `~ c^0.694` (`(log α)/(log 2)`,
`α` golden ratio) and note the exact minimal state counts for small `c` are tabulated as
OEIS A382296, but no closed-form / good formula is known.

**Why in fragment.** `t` is the canonical 2-automatic sequence (uniform morphism
`0->01,1->10`); `(t(i+c))` is a shift, itself 2-automatic by Cobham; "minimal DFAO state
count as a function of c" is exactly a `def`/minimize/`mem` sweep in our engine, msd, no
`learnfe` even needed — just build `t`, shift by `c`, minimize, record `|Q|`, for `c` up
to whatever budget allows, then curve-fit / OEIS-diff like `TARGET1.md`'s blowup sweep.

**Formula sketch.** Build `T` (Thue-Morse DFAO, 2 states, msd), then for a range of `c`:
`def Tc i "T[i+c]=1"` (or equivalent shift-by-constant), minimize, record `|Q|(c)`;
compare against A382296, extend past whatever `c` OEIS stops at, fit against `c^0.694`
lower bound and candidate closed forms (binary-weight of `c`, run-length structure of
`c` in base 2, etc. — Thue-Morse's shift-automaton state count is known to correlate
with the binary carry/borrow structure of `c`).

**Why stalled.** Purely a "nobody has run the sweep and looked for the pattern far
enough, or proved a closed form once found" problem — the authors give a bound and a
short OEIS table and stop. No conceptual obstruction stated.

**Difficulty guess.** Low-to-medium: getting the sequence of exact values for `c` up to
~10^4-10^5 is cheap (2-state seed, msd minimization); finding/proving the formula is the
real work but the shape (something in `c`'s binary digits) is a normal automatic-sequences
argument once the data is in hand.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-1.md`.** Still open. Since this entry was
written, R. Biswas (Aug 2026) proved the `c = 2^r` case (`a(2^r) = 2F_{r+3}`). Our run
extended the table to `c < 262144` (21x the OEIS b-file), proved a general offset-set
characterisation of the minimal msd DFAO for every `c`, and found strong computational
evidence that **no** closed form of the expected shape exists: the 2-kernel of A382296 has
rank `2^{e+1}-1` up to `e = 8`, so the sequence is not 2-regular and admits no
transfer-matrix formula over the binary digits of `c`. New (unproved) family laws and a
sharp general lower bound `a(c) >= 2F_{floor(log2 c)+3}` are recorded there.

**STATUS 2026-08-17 (refereed) — `paper/attack1-verdict.md`: PROVED.** Theorems 1-3 and
the corollary algorithm hold as stated and the 262 144-value table reproduces exactly
under two from-scratch reimplementations. The referee found and the doc now corrects: an
unsound "argmax converges to a fixed infinite word" claim in F4 (retracted; the LCP
sequence is not monotone), an undersold constant in F1 (`3.708`, not `2.342`, is the
constant actually supported by the data), a stale engine-limitation paragraph (fixed in
commit `63ad97e`), and missing attribution to MRS Theorem 9(b)/19 (the prior art Theorem 1
supersedes — Theorem 19 is itself false at `c=1`). Open Problem 2 remains open.

## 2. A family matching the linear-subsequence state-complexity upper bound — Open Problem 1, same paper

**Source.** Same paper, §4, "Open Problem 1" (just before Open Problem 2 above).

**Statement (verbatim).** "Find a class of automatic sequences `(h_m(i))_{i>=0}`,
generated by a DFAO of `m` states with msd-first input, such that there is a constant `C`
so the linear subsequences `(h_m(ni))_{i>=0}` have at least the state complexity `C n m^2`
for infinitely many `n`."

**Why in fragment.** Upper bound proved in the paper (their Corollary 10 / Theorem 1
chain: state complexity of `(h(ni+c))` is `<= k n m^2` via the interior-sequence subword
complexity bound `ρ_x(n) <= k n ρ_M̂(2) <= k n m^2`). They "do not know if [this] is
tight" — a matching-family search, structurally identical to Khodier's Open Problem 1(A)
that motivated our own `TARGET1.md` sweep (find a family realizing a known upper bound,
not just prove the bound).

**Formula sketch.** Sweep over small DFAOs `M` (m = 2..8, alphabet k = 2,3), for each
compute `h_m` and, for a range of `n`, minimal-DFA-size of the linear subsequence
`(h_m(ni))`; look for the ratio `|min DFA|/(n m^2)` staying bounded away from 0 as
`n -> infinity` (candidate: sequences whose interior automaton `M̂` has subword complexity
close to its own `k n m^2` ceiling — i.e. reuse `TARGET1.md`'s Finding 2 predictors,
subword-complexity growth rate, right-special count).

**Why stalled.** Same shape as TARGET1(A): the paper proves the bound and stops; no
family search was reported. This is exactly our `explore/blowup.py`-style sweep, just
against a different automaton (`(h(ni))` minimal size) instead of FE.

**Difficulty guess.** Medium: needs an automated sweep + minimization at scale `n`
possibly large (state complexity target `~n m^2` means `n` must range fairly wide to see
the asymptotic), otherwise same machinery as TARGET1.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-2.md`.** Solved. The generalized Thue-Morse
family `h_m(i) = s_2(i) mod m` answers Open Problem 1 with `C = 1/2` for every odd `n>=3`
(and `C -> 1-1/(3m) >= 5/6` along a subsequence), via a Nerode-class lemma (any
digit-additive sequence, any `n`) plus a reachability lemma proved here from Gelfond's
1968 equidistribution theorem, composed with the known Tromp-Shallit/Starosta factor-
complexity formula.

**STATUS 2026-08-17 (refereed) — `paper/attack2-verdict.md`: PROVED.** Confirmed correct
and re-verified independently well outside the document's own test range; also confirmed
still open in Moradi's April-2026 MMath thesis (UWSpace `10012/23044`). The referee notes
this is an assembly of three known pieces rather than new hard math, and the doc now adds:
the zero-consistency hypothesis (`delta(q0,0)=q0`) required by the window construction
throughout, a softened §5(i) ("a group automaton always attains the max", not uniquely —
42 maximisers at `k=3,m=3,n=7`, 18 of them group automata), and the `p=0` half-integer
edge case at `n=3`.

## 3. General Clergyman's Conjecture / "rusty numbers" — Fokkink & Joshi 2025

**Source.** R. Fokkink, G. Joshi, *Anti-recurrence sequences*, arXiv:2506.13337 (13 Jun
2025), Conjecture 4 + Theorem 12 + "Final remark". Builds on W. Bosma, R. Bruin, R.
Fokkink, J. Grube, A. Reuijl, T. Tromp, *Using Walnut to solve problems from the OEIS*,
arXiv:2503.04122, Conjecture 2 ("The Clergyman's Conjecture").

**Statement (verbatim, Conjecture 4).** "Let `a = (a_1,...,a_k)` be positive and integral
of dimension `k>1`. Let `A_n` be the anti-recurrence sequence for the linear form
`f(x)=a.x`. Then `A_n - κn` is `τ`-automatic for `κ = kτ+1` and `τ` the trace of the
linear form." (`A_n` = mex-based "anti-recurrence" sequence: sum of `k` consecutive
"missing numbers" of the complementary sequence to `a`.)

Proved (Theorem 12) only when `a` is *A1-bounded* (`A_1 <= (k-1)τ+2`). Left explicitly
open: **general `a`**, with the named unresolved case being the "rusty numbers" —
`a=(1,d)`, i.e. anti-recurrence for `X_{n+1}=dX_n+X_{n-1}` (metallic-number recurrence),
which is *not* A1-bounded for `d>=3`. Quote: "Surprisingly, it is a challenge to prove,
or disprove, the conjecture for the rusty numbers." A guessed 4-DFAO for the difference
sequence of the 4-rusty numbers matches an arithmetic-progression subsequence only up to
`n=348` (`A_{1741} != 9+55*348`), i.e. the naive automatic-sequence guess empirically
*breaks* — open whether a bigger/different automaton rescues it or the conjecture is
false there. Final remark also asks the general complementary-sequences (Fraenkel) version.

**Why in fragment.** Once a candidate DFAO for the difference sequence `A_n - κn` is
guessed, "is this automaton correct" is exactly a `learnfe`-style self-verifying-predicate
check (Khodier thesis §3, our `docs/LEARNFE.md`): a recurrence in `n` relating `A_n` to
`A_{n-1}` via the mex/complementary-sequence definition, checkable by `?`/`witness`
without building an intermediate blowup automaton.

**Formula sketch.** Define the anti-recurrence sequence via its defining recurrence
(complement of `{f(x): x in prior terms}`, greedy mex) as a `let`-predicate; guess a DFAO
for `A_n - κn` for `a=(1,3)`, `(1,4)`, `(1,5)` by active learning against the recurrence
(same technique as `learnfe`); if a fixed-size guess keeps failing past some `n` (as the
paper's hand-guess did at `n=1741`), grow the state budget / alphabet and re-run — this is
the same "small forward cap -> fails -> bigger cap" ladder already in the engine
(`TARGET1.md` "Construction" section) applied to a different target predicate.

**Why stalled.** Explicit "we are unable to settle the full conjecture" + a documented,
reproducible failure of the natural guess-and-verify approach at a small, checkable index
(`n=1741`) — reads as under-resourced compute/search, not a proven obstruction.

**Difficulty guess.** Medium: defining the anti-recurrence sequence's recurrence inside
our command language is the fiddly part (mex over a growing "used sums" set); once framed
as a `let` predicate, it's squarely a `learnfe`-shaped problem.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-3.md`.** Conjecture 4 remains open *in general*,
but the named obstruction is gone: the rusty numbers `a=(1,d)` are machine-proved
`tau`-automatic for `d = 3..12`, including `d = 4`, the case whose natural guess the paper
reports breaking at `n = 1741`. Our 9-state base-5 DFAO for `a=(1,4)` reproduces
`A_1741 = 19148` and explains the anomaly (one state has `delta(q,0) != q0`, first reached
at `n = 348`). In total ~250 linear forms across `k = 2,3,4` were verified by an
11-obligation induction discharged by the engine, ~60 of them not `A1`-bounded and so
outside Theorem 12. Two structural findings: the alphabet-window bound survives `A1`-
boundedness failing (observed `<= 2tau-2` everywhere) but the `2tau-1` *state* bound is
only claimed under `A1`-boundedness and does not extend past it (`a=(1,6)`: 16 states,
bound 13 applies only to `A1`-bounded forms). No uniform-in-`a` argument was found.

**STATUS 2026-08-17 (refereed) — `paper/attack3-verdict.md`: MACHINE-VERIFIED.** The
referee found one real gap: the `LAST` predicate in `explore/attack3_general.py` and
`docs/ATTACK-3.md` §3 was inverted (`G_g(m,y+k)` asserts `y=x-k`, not `y=x+k`), making
proof obligations P5/P6 vacuous and leaving a step of the §4 induction unsupported. Fixed
(one-line correction, both doc and script) and all 249 records re-verified clean
(`paper/verdict-attack3/rerun_fixed.jsonl`); the result and every table in §6 are
unchanged. The referee also corrected an overstated Observation 3 (no published theorem
is contradicted — Lemma 11/Theorem 12 are hypothesised on `A1`-boundedness, and every
`A1`-bounded form in the sweep does satisfy them) and updated the citation: the paper is
now published as *Integers* 26 (2026) #A24, renumbering Conjecture 4 -> Conjecture 2,
Theorem 12 -> Theorem 4, Definition 9 -> Definition 1, Lemma 11 -> Lemma 5.

## 4. Greedy 3-subsumfree meta-conjecture — Conjecture 6 of Bosma et al. 2025

**Source.** W. Bosma, R. Bruin, R. Fokkink, J. Grube, A. Reuijl, T. Tromp, *Using Walnut
to solve problems from the OEIS*, arXiv:2503.04122 (6 Mar 2025), §4.

**Statement (verbatim, Conjecture 6, "meta-Conjecture").** "Let `d>=2`. For every `g>=d+1`
the greedy 3-subsumfree sequence `S_{1,g,g+d}` is characterized as follows: `z in
S_{1,g,g+d} <=> z in {1,g,2g+d-1,2g+d}` or `z > g+d` and `z mod (5g+2d) in
{g+d-2,...,2g+d-2}`. In particular ... after the first `g+3` entries the sequence
`S_{1,g,g+d}` modulo `5g+2d` is periodic with period `g+1`." (`S_{x,y,z}` = greedy sequence
starting `x<y<z` where each `a_n` is the least integer exceeding `a_{n-1}` not a sum of 3
distinct earlier terms.)

Individually verified for many `(g,d)` pairs by Walnut (each fixed `g,d` gives a fixed
modulus, pure Presburger, trivial to check) and for the whole family by an independent
Magma computation, but the *uniform-in-`g`* statement is explicitly blocked: "The problem
here is that the definition of `isinGmodm` is not allowed in Walnut [because] the operator
`*` cannot be applied to two variables." I.e. `k*(10g+3)` with both `k` and `g`
variables is outside quantifier-free Presburger as Walnut accepts it.

**Why in fragment.** Every fixed instance is Presburger/ultimately-periodic (hence
trivially automatic in any base); the obstruction is purely syntactic (can't multiply two
free variables) — a tooling/bookkeeping wall, not a math one, exactly the kind of thing
worth checking against our own engine's constraint set (`mem`/`def`/`?` — does our
Presburger front end have the same restriction? if not, this may already be directly
attackable).

**Formula sketch.** Either (a) check whether our engine allows `k*(5*g+2*d)`-style terms
with two free variables at all (likely not — same Presburger restriction), in which case
attack via the `learnfe` self-verifying-recurrence trick instead: define `isinG(g,d,z)` by
an inductive recurrence in `g` (not a closed-form modulus) and verify the recurrence
holds for all `g,d,z` without ever materializing the multiplication; or (b) brute-force
verify many more `(g,d)` pairs (their Walnut runs only went to `g<=10`) to build
confidence / look for a counterexample.

**Why stalled.** Authors state the exact syntactic blocker and stop there; this reads as
solvable by reformulation rather than new mathematics.

**Difficulty guess.** Medium — reformulating a parametrized-modulus periodicity claim as
a variable-multiplication-free recurrence is a real (if mechanical) piece of work.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-4.md`.** Solved. The blocker is removed by a
change of variables (carry every integer as its `(quotient, remainder)` pair against
`m = 5g+2d`; the single product `k*m` then only ever appears as `j*m` with `j in {0,1,2}`
a constant, by a three-case carry split of `a+b+c = z`). The resulting Presburger
sentences prove **Conjecture 17 (= arXiv Conjecture 6) for every `d>=2, g>=d+1`**, and in
fact prove the sharp statement: the conjectured description equals `S_{1,g,g+d}` **iff**
`d>=2`, `g>=d` and `2g>=d+3` — one column further left than conjectured (the diagonal
`g=d`, `d>=3`, which includes the paper's own worked Example 19, `S_{1,4,8}`). A
pen-and-paper interval-sumset proof in Shtrezi's style is given as well. Note the `d=1`
companion (Conjecture 16) was proved by O. Shtrezi, arXiv:2606.17447 (Jun 2026); the
meta-conjecture was not touched there. Peanut: 8 sentences, 6.2 s, peak 1.3 GB;
independently re-proved in Walnut 8-dev (52.7 s) — the reformulation, not the engine, is
what does the work.

## 5. Efficient synchronization-delay algorithm for k-automatic sequences — Khodier thesis Open Problem 4 (uniform-morphism case)

**Source.** M. Khodier, *New Methods for Analyzing the Properties of Automatic
Sequences*, MMath thesis, U. Waterloo, 19 Jan 2026 (`docs/khodier2026-thesis.pdf`), Ch. 8,
Open Problem 4.

**Statement (verbatim, restricted to the in-fragment half).** "Find an efficient
algorithm for computing the synchronization delay of ... k-automatic sequences." The
thesis gives a Walnut-based reformulation (indices/lengths instead of raw words) for a
binary k-uniform morphism (their 3-adic Tribonacci-doubling-complement example) and
reports it as "already very inefficient even for uniform morphisms" — one sub-predicate
(`eq1`, "check `v1 u v2 = h(s)`") alone costs 3053 states, and the full `synchpoint`
predicate nests four quantifier alternations over these.

**Why in fragment.** The uniform-morphism half of Open Problem 4 (drop the non-uniform-
morphism and Fibonacci/Tribonacci-numeration extensions the thesis also asks for) is pure
`<N,+,V_k>`: synchronization delay is an FO property of a k-uniform-morphism DFAO, exactly
our engine's territory, and the thesis's own worked example is a standard base-3 automatic
sequence (msd_3), not a non-standard numeration system.

**Formula sketch.** Reformulate synchronization-delay as a `learnfe`-style self-verifying
recurrence the way `docs/LEARNFE.md` did for equality-of-factors: synchronization delay
of a synchronized/automatic relation has a known recursive characterization in terms of
one-step morphism unfolding; guess-and-verify against that recurrence should sidestep the
4-quantifier-alternation blowup the direct Walnut encoding hits, the same way `learnfe`
sidesteps FE's blowup.

**Why stalled.** Thesis explicitly frames it as an inefficiency problem ("the problem is
that it is already very inefficient") with a concrete 3053-state data point, not a
conceptual gap — and it is the closest open problem in the literature to our engine's
demonstrated strength (avoiding intermediate-automaton blowup via self-verification).

**Difficulty guess.** Medium-high: needs the correct recurrence for synchronization delay
(analogous to LEARNFE.md's (R) for FE) worked out first; construction/engine work after
that should be routine given `learnfe` already exists.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-5.md`.** Answered for the in-fragment half.
The delay is *not* a `learnfe`-style recurrence problem: Theorem A there proves that a
factor `u` of the fixed point of a `k`-uniform morphism has a synchronizing point iff all
its occurrences are congruent mod `k` **and** the implied cut position `kappa` is `<= |u|`,
so the whole question is three quantifiers on top of the existing `FE` automaton. TDC's
delay comes out in a 4-state automaton and unmeasurable time, against the thesis's `rhs`
predicate (14 free variables), which fails here too (8.3 GB) exactly as it failed in
Walnut; the thesis's five reported state counts (4/92/291/291/3053) all reproduce
exactly. New: exhaustive exact maxima of `Z_min` over *all* binary `k`-uniform morphisms
for `k = 2..7` (3, 6, 19, 20, 76, 42 — 11 163 morphisms decided, 0 brute-force
mismatches); two families with closed forms `k(k-1)` and `k(k-1)^2/2+1` giving the first
matching lower bounds for Klouda-Medkova's 2016 upper bounds (TCS 615, prior art the
thesis does not cite) -- within `1+4/k` of them for odd prime `k`, same cubic order for
even `k`, at every `k` tested; the extension of the notion to *coded* `k`-automatic
sequences with `L(tau(w)) >= L(w)`; and three errata in the thesis (a missing
`kappa <= |u|` clause in `circulartdc`, Frid's uncircularity case (iii) transcribed
without its `h(1)=1^k` hypothesis -- which as printed declares Thue-Morse uncircular --
and a wrong length equation in the Open Problem 4 encoding). Still open: any general upper bound for alphabets of
size > 2, an algorithm polynomial in the DFAO size rather than in `|FE|`, and the whole
non-uniform-morphism half.

Note: the thesis is a **Master of Mathematics** thesis, not a PhD thesis (corrected in
the source line above).

## 6. Existence of a k-automatic word with unbounded-sum winning shift — Question 10.1, Peltomäki & Salo 2022

**Source.** J. Peltomäki, V. Salo, *Automatic winning shifts*, arXiv:2106.07249 (v2, 17
Feb 2022; Information and Computation 2022), §10, Question 10.1.

**Statement (verbatim).** "Is there an addable ANS `S` such that some `S`-automatic word
has a winning shift with unbounded sums?" (An abstract numeration system `S` is addable
if `+` is `S`-automatic; the ordinary base-`k` numeration system is the simplest addable
ANS, so "k-automatic word" is squarely inside this question, not a generalization past
it.) They note: "If the answer is negative, then one can drop the assumption of sublinear
complexity in Theorem 6.2" — i.e. resolving even the base-`k` special case has direct
theoretical payoff.

**Why in fragment.** The winning shift `W(X)` of a subshift `X` generated by a k-automatic
word, restricted to a fixed window length, is itself computed and manipulated as a DFA
in the paper's own worked examples (§6) — "unbounded sums" is a property of that DFA
family (does the maximum row-sum grow without bound as window length grows), decidable
per-window and searchable computationally.

**Formula sketch.** Take small uniform-morphism DFAOs (Thue-Morse-scale, m<=8, k=2,3),
construct the corresponding winning-shift automaton for increasing window lengths per
the paper's §6 recipe, and track the max sum statistic; look for growth vs. boundedness,
same sweep-and-classify approach as `TARGET1.md`.

**Why stalled.** Paper explicitly flags it as future computational work: "it would be of
interest to try to extend the practical computations in Section 6 to examples where the
winning shift has larger coding dimension. We expect that the methods scale very badly,
but this intuition has often turned out to be wrong in [Walnut-style] automatic
theorem-proving." I.e. the authors themselves predict this is a "just try it, it might be
easier than it looks" problem.

**Difficulty guess.** Medium-high: requires implementing the winning-shift construction
(not currently in our engine) before any sweep is possible — real up-front engineering
cost, but the payoff is a clean existence/nonexistence answer to a named open question.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-6.md`.** **This entry is mis-scoped.** Question
10.1 is *not* open for base `k`: every `k`-automatic word has factor complexity `O(n)`
(Cobham 1972), so Prop. 6.1 of the same paper gives its winning shift finite coding
dimension — and the paper states that conclusion outright as Corollary 6.3 for every Pisot
numeration system, base `k` included. A positive answer needs an addable *non-Pisot* ANS
(the paper's own Prop. 6.9 kills its only superlinear-complexity example, and §3.1 says no
addable non-Pisot radix ANS is known), which is a numeration-system question, outside
`<N,+,V_k>`. The in-fragment part is the section's *final remark* ("extend the practical
computations ... to examples where the winning shift has larger coding dimension"), and
that was attacked instead: all four published coding dimensions reproduced (Thue-Morse 3,
period-doubling 2, paperfolding 3, Rudin-Shapiro 4) in under a second each where Walnut
had run out of memory on the direct formula; the record pushed from **4 to 10**; a
recursion with **one** positional variable per level instead of the paper's `2^{d-2}`; the
effective bound `D(x) <= 1 + log2 sup_n s_x(n)`; a proved family of binary 2-automatic
words with `D >= r` for every `r` (so the coding dimension is unbounded over the class
though finite for each word); and a machine-verified off-by-one correction to the
published description of `W(X)` for the period-doubling word. An engine bug was found and
fixed on the way (`Dfao::build_lsd` truncated the state count to `u8`, so every DFAO with
`>= 256` states was unusable).

## 7. Effective procedure for winning-shift descriptions of uniform-morphism fixed points — Problem 10.2, same paper

**Source.** Same as #6, Problem 10.2.

**Statement (verbatim).** "Devise an effective procedure that, given a fixed point `x` of
a substitution `τ` and a word `v`, computes a finite description of `P_v(W(X))` where `X`
is the subshift generated by `x`." Restricted to `τ` k-uniform (drop the general
substitution / non-uniform case), this is in-fragment.

**Why in fragment.** For `τ` k-uniform, `x` is exactly a k-automatic sequence in our
sense; `P_v(W(X))` is defined and computed by hand for small worked cases in the paper's
§6 (coding-dimension-bounded cases only) — asking for "an effective procedure" is asking
to automate what the paper currently does by hand per example.

**Why stalled.** Authors: "For a general substitutive subshift we do not know how to
describe even the bounded sum restrictions of winning shifts in general," immediately
followed by the practical-computation remark quoted in #6 — same "scales badly in theory,
untested in practice" framing.

**Difficulty guess.** High: this is "write the general algorithm," not "run a sweep" —
closer to genuine new construction work, ranked below #6 for that reason even though it's
the more general ask.

**ATTACKED 2026-08-17 — see `docs/ATTACK-7.md`.** For `k`-uniform substitutions the
procedure now exists, is proved and is implemented for **arbitrary `v`** (arbitrary branch
degrees, arbitrary alphabet), not just the binary `v = 1^d` case that `docs/ATTACK-6.md`
covered the same day: a ladder of `let`-predicates whose arity is linear in the number of
branchings, against `2^{d-2}` free positions in the source's own hand-written encoding and
`prod_t(v_t+1)` in its Theorem 5.5 formula (both re-run here for comparison; the latter
reproduces the authors' Walnut out-of-memory already at `d = 3`). The paper's §7 is
reproduced at figure level (useful-state counts 8/6/14/17 = the node counts of Figures
2-5), closed forms are given for the paperfolding and Rudin-Shapiro winning shifts (the
source and ATTACK-6 give only automata), and exact coding dimensions are computed over
non-binary alphabets, including **7** for the Thue-Morse x Rudin-Shapiro product, proved
exact by deciding all 176 words `v` with `sum(v) <= 8`. Problem 10.2 for **non-uniform**
substitutions (Dumont-Thomas) is untouched and remains open.

## 8. Characterize which formulas admit a self-verifying predicate — Khodier thesis Open Problem 2

**Source.** Khodier thesis (as above), Ch. 8, Open Problem 2.

**Statement (verbatim).** "Give a characterization for the logical formulas that are
self-verifying predicates." Lists four known-to-work instances (periods of factors,
equality of reversals, numeration-system adders, summation of synchronized sequences) and
states "no general theory exists."

**Why in fragment (partial).** This is a statement *about* the FO fragment (which FO
formulas over `<N,+,V_k>` admit a Khodier/learnfe-style recurrence with a unique fixed
point) rather than an FO sentence itself — meta-level, so weaker fit than #1-#7, but it
is the precise open question behind our own `learnfe` machinery (`docs/LEARNFE.md`), and
every new success/failure of `learnfe` on a new target is a data point toward answering it.

**Why stalled.** Explicitly "no general theory exists" — genuinely unstarted rather than
compute-blocked; better framed as "accumulate `learnfe` case studies" than "run a sweep."

**Difficulty guess.** High / open-ended (this is a research program, not a single
target) — ranked last of the substantive items for that reason.
