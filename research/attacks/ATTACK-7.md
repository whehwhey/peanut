## 1. The statement, verbatim

> **Problem 10.2.** Devise an effective procedure that, given a fixed point `x` of a
> substitution `τ` and a word `v`, computes a finite description of `P_v(W(X))` where
> `X` is the subshift generated by `x`.

Context in the source, also verbatim:

> "For a general substitutive subshift we do not know how to describe even the bounded
> sum restrictions of winning shifts in general. It seems natural to guess that the
> winning shift is somehow 'substitutive' in this case too …"

> "An obvious candidate for a finite description would be to show recognizability with
> respect to the corresponding Dumont-Thomas numeration system for `P_v(W(X))`, but we
> do not see why this would hold."

> "Besides these theoretical problems, it would be of interest to try to extend the
> practical computations in Section 6 to examples where the winning shift has larger
> coding dimension. We expect that the methods scale very badly, but this intuition has
> often turned out to be wrong in the setting of automatic theorem-proving."

(The computations meant are in **§7** of arXiv v2, "Automata for the Winning Shifts of
Certain Automatic Words"; §10 calls it Section 6. This document says §7.)

And, on the state of those computations (§7, first paragraph):

> "In our attempt to directly input the formula of Theorem 5.5, Walnut quickly ran out of
> memory even in the case of one of the simplest automatic words, the Thue-Morse word."

### Definitions used here

`A` a finite alphabet, `x ∈ A^N`, `X = O(x)` the subshift it generates, `L(X)` its
language (= the factors of `x`). A **choice sequence** is `y ∈ N^N`; Alice and Bob play
`|y|` rounds, on round `j` Alice offers a set `A_j ⊆ A` with `|A_j| = y_j + 1` and Bob
picks `a_j ∈ A_j`; Alice wins if the built word lies in `L(X)` (in `X`, for the
infinite game). `W(X)` is the set of `y`
for which Alice has a winning strategy — the **winning shift**. For a word
`v ∈ N_{>0}^d`, `P_v(W(X)) = { (n_1 < … < n_d) : the y with y[n_t] = v_t and 0 elsewhere
lies in W(X) }` (source, Def. 5.1). The **coding dimension** of `W(X)` is
`max { Σy : y ∈ W(X) }` (Def. 4.2). Since `W(X)` is hereditary and shift-invariant, the
family `{P_v(W(X))}_v` *is* `W(X)`.

Throughout, `τ` is `k`-uniform, so `x` is `k`-automatic and the Dumont-Thomas system of
`τ` is ordinary base `k`: "finite description" = a base-`k` DFA over `d` tracks.

## 2. What was already known, honestly

* **Per-`v` effectiveness is already in the source paper for this restriction.**
  Theorem 5.5 writes `P_v(W(X))` as a first-order formula over `S`-recognizable
  predicates; for base `k` the Büchi–Bruyère theorem turns that into a DFA, and the
  authors say so: "The proof of the main result is constructive, so in principle an
  automaton for the winning shift can be found algorithmically." What Problem 10.2 asks
  for beyond that is the *general substitution* case (non-uniform, non-Pisot), which is
  outside this repo's fragment and is **not** claimed here.
* **Marked uniform substitutions** already have a structural description:
  Peltomäki–Salo, *On winning shifts of marked uniform substitutions*, RAIRO-ITA 53
  (2019) (arXiv:1705.08747), Theorem 4.9 — long choice sequences in `W(τ)` are images of
  short ones under `σ(k) = k1^{M-1}`. That covers Thue-Morse and the period-doubling
  word but not every uniform substitution (markedness is used for desubstitution).
* **The binary case of the machinery below, in this repo** — `docs/ATTACK-6.md`
  (commits `14fe191`, `b51ad89`, `8c21590`, 2026-08-17): the one-positional-variable
  recursion for `P_{1^d}`, the bound `D <= 1 + log2 sup_n s_x(n)`, the four published
  coding dimensions reproduced, binary examples of coding dimension 5, 6 and 10, and the
  period-doubling erratum of §5.2 below. Everything in the present document that concerns
  binary alphabets and `v = 1^d` should be read as *that* attack's result.
* **Four worked examples** are in §7 of the target paper: Thue-Morse (coding
  dimension 3), period-doubling (2), regular paperfolding (3), Rudin-Shapiro (4), each
  as a hand-drawn automaton (Figures 2-5), with human-readable characterizations printed
  for Thue-Morse and period-doubling only. They were obtained not from Theorem 5.5 but
  from a **hand-written ladder** (`extRS2`, `extRS3`, `extRS4` in their §7 Walnut listing),
  written out by hand for `d ≤ 4`, for binary alphabets and `v = 1^d` only.

So the gap this document addresses is: *a single procedure, uniform in `v` and in the
alphabet, that is practical enough to run* — plus the extension of the §7 computations
that the authors ask for.

## 3. The procedure

Fix `v = v_1 … v_d ∈ N_{>0}^d` and write `c_t = v_t + 1` for the branch degrees.

**Lemma 1 (strategy trees).** Let `0 ≤ i_1 < … < i_d` and let `y` be the configuration
with `y[i_t] = v_t` and `0` elsewhere. Then `y ∈ W(X)` iff there is a *strategy tree*: a
rooted tree of depth `i_d + 1` whose edges are labelled by letters of `A`, in which every
node at depth `i_t` has exactly `c_t` children with pairwise distinct labels, every other
node at depth `< i_d+1` has exactly one child, and every root-to-leaf word lies in `L(X)`.

*Proof.* (⇐) Alice offers, at round `j`, the label set of the children of the node reached
so far; after round `i_d` the word built is in `L(X)`. From round `i_d+1` on `y_j = 0`, so
she offers one letter, and since every factor of `x` extends to the right inside `L(X)`
she can continue forever; the resulting infinite word has all factors in `L(X)`, hence
lies in the (closed, shift-invariant) `X`. (⇒) A winning strategy restricted to the first
`i_d+1` rounds is such a tree: Alice must offer exactly `y_j+1` distinct letters at round
`j`, and every play is a prefix of a point of `X`, hence in `L(X)`. ∎

Write `FE(m, m', l)` for "`x[m .. m+l-1] = x[m' .. m'+l-1]`" (the engine's `learnfe`
predicate) and define, for `t = d, d-1, …, 1`, predicates `B_t ⊆ N^{d-t+2}`:

```
B_d(i_d, m)          :=  E m_2..m_{c_d}.  /\_r FE(m, m_r, i_d)
                                        & pairwise-distinct( x[m+i_d], x[m_2+i_d], …, x[m_{c_d}+i_d] )

B_t(i_t,…,i_d, m)    :=  i_t < i_{t+1}
                       & E m_2..m_{c_t}.  /\_r FE(m, m_r, i_t)
                                        & pairwise-distinct( x[m+i_t], x[m_2+i_t], …, x[m_{c_t}+i_t] )
                                        & /\_{r=1..c_t} B_{t+1}(i_{t+1},…,i_d, m_r)      (m_1 := m)
```

**Theorem 1** (the `v = 1^d` binary case is ATTACK-6 Prop. 1). For all `0 ≤ i_1 < … < i_d`,

    (i_1,…,i_d) ∈ P_v(W(X))   <=>   E m. B_1(i_1,…,i_d, m).

*Proof.* Call a word `w ∈ L(X)` of length `i_t` **`t`-good** if there is a strategy tree
of depth `i_d+1` that branches at depths `i_t,…,i_d` with degrees `c_t,…,c_d` and whose
initial (unbranched) path spells `w`. We prove, by downward induction on `t`:

  (a) `B_t(i_t,…,i_d, m)` implies `x[m .. m+i_t-1]` is `t`-good;
  (b) if `x[m .. m+i_t-1]` is `t`-good then there is `m'` with `FE(m, m', i_t)` and
      `B_t(i_t,…,i_d, m')`.

*Base `t = d`.* `B_d(i_d,m)` says the length-`i_d` factor at `m` has at least `c_d`
distinct right extensions inside `L(X)`, which is exactly a depth-`i_d+1` tree branching
`c_d` ways at depth `i_d`; and `m' = m` serves in (b).

*Step.* Assume (a),(b) for `t+1`.
(a) Let `m_1 = m, m_2, …, m_{c_t}` witness `B_t`. By the induction hypothesis (a) each
`u_r := x[m_r .. m_r+i_{t+1}-1]` is `(t+1)`-good. All `u_r` share the length-`i_t` prefix
`w = x[m..m+i_t-1]` (by `FE`) and their letters `a_r = x[m_r+i_t]` are pairwise distinct.
Glue: stem `w`, then `c_t` children labelled `a_1,…,a_{c_t}`, below child `r` the rest of
`u_r` followed by the `(t+1)`-good tree of `u_r`. That is a strategy tree for `w`, so `w`
is `t`-good.
(b) Let `T` witness that `w` is `t`-good. Its `c_t` children at depth `i_t` carry distinct
letters `a_1,…,a_{c_t}`, and below child `r` the path to depth `i_{t+1}` spells a word
`u_r ∈ L(X)` of length `i_{t+1}` with prefix `w a_r`, which is `(t+1)`-good. Pick `p_r`
with `u_r = x[p_r .. p_r+i_{t+1}-1]`; by the induction hypothesis (b) there is `m_r` with
`FE(p_r, m_r, i_{t+1})` and `B_{t+1}(…, m_r)`. Because `i_t < i_{t+1}`, every `m_r` again
has length-`i_t` prefix `w` and letter `a_r` at offset `i_t`. Then `m' := m_1` satisfies
`FE(m,m',i_t)` and `B_t(i_t,…,i_d,m')` with witnesses `m_2,…,m_{c_t}`.

Finally `E m. B_1(…, m)` iff some length-`i_1` factor is `1`-good (by (a),(b)) iff a
strategy tree exists, iff, by Lemma 1, `(i_1,…,i_d) ∈ P_v(W(X))`. ∎

**Corollary (the effective procedure).** `FE` and `x[·]` are `k`-automatic predicates, so
every `B_t` and `P_v` is definable in `<N,+,V_k>` extended by `x`, hence computable as a
minimal base-`k` DFA. The arity of `B_t` is `d-t+2`, and building `B_t` from `B_{t+1}`
adds `c_t - 1` existential tracks: the whole ladder is **linear in `d`**, in contrast with
the `∏_t c_t` position variables of the source's Theorem 5.5 formula and the `2^{d-2}`
free position variables of its hand-written `extRS` ladder.

**Proposition 2 (the whole of `W(X)`, effectively; binary case = ATTACK-6 Cor. 2).** Enumerate `v` by layers of
increasing `Σv`. Since `W(X)` is hereditary (Salo–Törmä), `{v : P_v ≠ ∅}` is closed
downwards under deleting a letter and under decrementing a letter, so once one entire
layer is empty every later layer is empty. For `k`-automatic `x` the coding dimension is
finite (source Prop. 6.1, using Cassaigne's theorem and the `O(n)` complexity of automatic
sequences), so an empty layer is reached: the loop terminates and returns a finite list of
nonempty `v` together with their automata — a finite description of `W(X)`, not merely of
one `P_v`. (For binary `x` one can stop earlier, at ATTACK-6's `1 + log2 R`. For a general
alphabet the layers are indexed by compositions, and the empty-layer criterion is what
makes the loop finite without needing a bound in advance.)

**Proposition 3 (a priori bound; binary case = ATTACK-6 Thm. 3, `2^{d-1} <= R`).** Let `s_x(n)` be the number of right-special factors of
length `n` and `S = sup_n s_x(n)` (finite, by the same Cassaigne argument). If
`P_v ≠ ∅` then

    prod_{t<d} (v_t + 1)  <=  s_x(i_d)  <=  S      and      v_d <= |A| - 1,

hence the coding dimension is at most `max { Σv : v satisfies both constraints }`.

*Proof.* In a strategy tree the nodes at depth `i_d` are `∏_{t<d} c_t` pairwise distinct
words of length `i_d` (two of them differ at the first branch depth separating them), and
each has at least `c_d ≥ 2` distinct right extensions in `L(X)`, i.e. is right special. ∎

## 4. How it is expressed in the engine

`explore/attack7_ws.py` emits exactly the ladder of §3 (`ladder_lines`). For
`v = 111` on Thue-Morse the whole script is

```
mode msd
def T 2 2 0 01 10 01
learnfe FE
let L3(i3,m) E m2. $FE(m,m2,i3) & T[m+i3]!=T[m2+i3]
let L2(i2,i3,m) i2<i3 & (E m2. $FE(m,m2,i2) & T[m+i2]!=T[m2+i2] & $L3(i3,m) & $L3(i3,m2))
let L1(i1,i2,i3,m) i1<i2 & (E m2. $FE(m,m2,i1) & T[m+i1]!=T[m2+i1] & $L2(i2,i3,m) & $L2(i2,i3,m2))
let PV(i1,i2,i3) E m. $L1(i1,i2,i3,m)
```

Two engine features carry the weight. `learnfe` supplies `factorEq` without ever
compiling `A t. t<l => T[i+t]=T[j+t]` (`docs/LEARNFE.md`) — and `factorEq` is precisely
the predicate the source paper's own encoding leans on. The `let`-ladder keeps every
intermediate automaton minimal, so the `E m_2` projections are run against small inputs.

### 5.2 The published characterizations: two confirmed, one corrected (independent confirmation of ATTACK-6 §3.5)

All four sentences below are closed and were decided by the engine over **all** of `N`
(`results/attack7_chars.json`). Positions are the paper's, 1-based.

* **Thue-Morse, three occurrences** (paper §7): `a = 1`, `c - b = 2^k` with `k ≥ 1`,
  `b - 1 ≤ 2^{k-1}`. As an iff (adding only `b > a`, i.e. the paper's own `a<b<c`):
  **TRUE**. The published condition is exactly right.
* **Thue-Morse, exactly two occurrences**: `b = 1`, or `c - b = 2^k` and `b-1 ≤ 2^{k-1}`.
  As an iff: **TRUE**.
* **Period-doubling, exactly two occurrences** (paper §7): "`b - a = 2^k` for some
  `k ≥ 1` and `a - 1 ≤ 2^{k-1}`". As an iff: **FALSE**. *This erratum is ATTACK-6 §3.5,
  found there first and with the same smallest counterexample `(a,b) = (2,4)`; what
  follows was derived independently here and agrees line for line, which is the point of
  recording it.* As an implication (⇒): **TRUE**.
  So the printed condition is necessary but *not* sufficient, and the section that
  introduces it does call the list a characterization.
  The **exact** condition is the same with a strict inequality,

      b - a = 2^k  (k >= 1)   and   a - 1 < 2^{k-1}   (equivalently a <= 2^{k-1}),

  machine-proved as an iff over all of `N`.
  Smallest counterexample to the published version: `(a,b) = (2,4)`, i.e. the choice
  sequence `0100^ω` with 1s at 1-based positions 2 and 4. It satisfies the printed
  condition (`b-a = 2 = 2^1`, `a-1 = 1 ≤ 2^0 = 1`) but is **not** in `W(X)`. Certificate,
  independent of everything else in this repo: the period-doubling word has exactly one
  right-special factor of length 3, namely `010` (its length-4 factors are `0001, 0010,
  0100, 0101, 1000, 1010`), and a strategy tree branching at depths 1 and 3 needs *two*
  distinct right-special factors of length 3 agreeing in position 0 and differing in
  position 1. The `⇐` direction of the Thue-Morse statement is unaffected — the two words
  genuinely differ here, `s_t(3) = 4` against `s_pd(3) = 1`.

### 5.3 New characterizations: paperfolding and Rudin-Shapiro

For these two the source gives an automaton picture (Figures 4, 5) and no closed form, and
ATTACK-6's ledger lists them under "not established" as well. Each is an iff
proved over all of `N`; `d := j - i`, positions 0-based (`i` = the first branch depth).

**Paperfolding, two branchings.**

    (i,j) in P_11(W(X))  <=>  d = 2^e (e >= 0) and i < 2d
                           or d = 3*2^e (e >= 1) and 3i + 3 <= d
                           or d in {3,5} and i <= 1.

**Paperfolding, three branchings.**

    (i,j,k) in P_111(W(X))  <=>  (E e) [ b = 2^e, i < b, j = i + b, k in {j+b, j+2b} ]
                              or (i,j,k) = (0,1,4).

**Rudin-Shapiro, two branchings.**

    (i,j) in P_11(W(X))  <=>  i = 0
                           or (d = 2^e and i <= 2d)
                           or (d = 3*2^e, e >= 1, and 3i <= d)
                           or (d in {3,5} and i <= 2).

The sporadic clauses (`d ∈ {3,5}`, and `(0,1,4)`) are genuine: dropping them makes the
iff false, and adding them to the general pattern makes it true. No closed form of
comparable size was found for `P_111`/`P_1111` of Rudin-Shapiro; those stay as automata
(14 and 9 states).

4-letter alphabet — still `2`-automatic (an 8-state DFAO), so squarely inside the target's
uniform-substitution setting.

Two caveats keep these numbers in proportion. (i) **They are not the record.** ATTACK-6
§3.2/§3.4 certifies coding dimension 10 for a binary 2-automatic word. (ii) **Large
alphabets get a head start**: as ATTACK-6 remarks, `(|A|-1)0^ω` is winning as soon as every
letter occurs, so any 4-letter example has coding dimension `>= 3` for free.

What these two examples do add is *exactness in the general-`v` setting*. The value 7 for
`t × r` is proved, not certified from below: all 176 words `v` over `{1,2,3}` with
`Σv <= 8` were decided by the ladder, 27 of them nonempty, and the **entire `Σv = 8` layer
is empty**, so Proposition 2 pins the coding dimension at exactly 7. (ATTACK-6's 6, 10 are
certified lower bounds whose matching upper bounds are brute force over levels `<= 30`, or
absent for `x_9`, `x_10`.) The maximal choice sequences have shape `v = 331` — a degree-4
branching, a second degree-4 branching, then a degree-2 branching, 32 leaves — which is
the kind of `v` that only exists once the alphabet is bigger than binary, and which the
`P_{1^d}` ladder cannot see at all.

The non-binary cases also show that the nonempty `v` are **not** determined by `Σv` or by
the multiset of letters: for `s_3(n) mod 3`, `P_{211} ≠ ∅` while `P_{121} = P_{112} = ∅`,
and for `t × r`, `P_{331} ≠ ∅` while `P_{313}` and `P_{133}` are empty. Heredity
(delete-or-decrement) is the only monotonicity available, and it is what Proposition 2
runs on.

Prop. 3's bound is attained in 7 of the 13 sequences and is never violated. The ternary
and product cases show it is not tight in general: the counting of right-special factors
ignores how they must nest.

## 6. Verification

Four independent layers; every number in §5 is covered by at least three.

1. **The engine's own logic.** Every `P_v` is a minimal DFA and every claim in §5.2-5.3
   is a closed sentence decided over all of `N` — not sampling. 34 such sentences,
   0 failures (`explore/attack7_chars.py`, `results/attack7_chars.json`).
2. **A from-scratch strategy-tree DP** (`explore/attack7_brute.py`, `tree_dp`): iterate
   the morphism in Python, collect the factor sets from a prefix (checked stable against
   a 3× longer prefix), and decide the tree existence by a dynamic program over *words*.
   Shares no code with the engine.
3. **The raw game** (`brute_pv_game`): play Alice-vs-Bob from the definition
   (`#{a : Alice wins from wa} ≥ α_j+1`) against the target set `L_n(X)`, for `n` two
   letters past the last branching. This checks Lemma 1 itself, not just the ladder.
4. **The rival encodings** of §4, machine-checked equivalent to the ladder wherever they
   are computable at all.

Layers 2 and 3 and the engine's own `enum` agree, as sets of tuples up to a per-arity
bound (64 for `d=1` down to 18 for `d=4`), on **100** `(sequence, v)` records: every `v`
with `Σv ≤ 5` for eleven sequences, plus ten hand-picked `v` for `t × r` (including the
maximal `331` and the empty `332`, `3311`) and seven for `t × p`. **Zero** mismatches
(`results/attack7_brute*.json`). Structural sanity is also machine-checked: shift
invariance (`P_v(i_1+1,…) ⇒ P_v(i_1,…)`) and heredity (deleting or decrementing a 1 stays
in `W(X)`) hold for every case tried, as they must for a hereditary subshift.

The one place where the engine is weaker than the prefix count is `S` itself: the
predicate "some length has `B` distinct right-special factors" costs `B+1` tracks and
exceeds 4 GB above `B = 6-7`, so `S_engine` in `results/attack7_bound.json` is a proved
*lower* bound for Rudin-Shapiro and the products, while `S_prefix` is a prefix count.
Nothing in §5 depends on `S`: Prop. 3 is used only as a sanity bound, and the coding
dimensions are established by the empty-layer argument of Prop. 2.

## 7. Ledger

**Known before this attack (not claimed here).**
* Per-`v` effectiveness for `k`-automatic `x` follows from the source's own Theorem 5.5
  plus Büchi-Bruyère, and the authors say the proof is constructive.
* `W(X)` is a hereditary subshift with the same factor complexity as `X` (Salo-Törmä
  2014); coding dimension is finite under sublinear complexity (source Prop. 6.1);
  Cobham 1972 and Cassaigne's theorem behind it.
* Marked uniform substitutions: substitutive structure of `W(X)` (Peltomäki-Salo 2019,
  Thm. 4.9).
* The coding dimensions 3, 2, 3, 4 and the automata for Thue-Morse, period-doubling,
  paperfolding, Rudin-Shapiro (source §7, Figures 2-5), and the Thue-Morse
  characterization confirmed in §5.2.
* **`docs/ATTACK-6.md` (same repo, 2026-08-17, concurrent):** the binary `v = 1^d`
  recursion (its Prop. 1), the bound `D <= 1 + log2 sup_n s_x(n)` (its Thm. 3), the binary
  whole-`W(X)` loop (its Cor. 2), binary coding dimensions 5, 6, 10, and the
  period-doubling erratum. This attack duplicates that work in the binary case; the
  duplication is real and is not counted as new below.

**New here.**
* **Theorem 1 in general form**: arbitrary `v ∈ N_{>0}^d`, arbitrary alphabet, hence
  branch degrees `v_t + 1 > 2`, with the two-directional (`t`-good) induction the general
  case needs. This is what Problem 10.2 literally asks for ("given … a word `v`"); the
  binary `v = 1^d` case is ATTACK-6's. Implemented for every `v` and both digit orders.
* **Propositions 2 and 3 in general form**: the empty-layer termination criterion over
  *compositions* (not just depths), and `∏_{t<d}(v_t+1) <= S` with `v_d <= |A|-1`.
* **Closed forms for the paperfolding and Rudin-Shapiro winning shifts** (§5.3), machine-
  proved as iff-sentences over all of `N`. ATTACK-6's ledger lists exactly this as *not
  established* ("we produce the `L_2`, `L_3` automata and their exact tuple lists, but
  extracted no formula"); the source paper gives only Figures 4 and 5.
* **Figure-level reproduction of the source's §7**: the whole-`W(X)` automaton in
  the authors' own `(a,b,c)` encoding, with useful-state counts 8, 6, 14, 17 matching the
  node counts of Figures 2-5 exactly (plus one dead state each), and their two worked
  encodings `(1,5,13)`, `(0,3,11)` accepted.
* **Measured cost of both published encodings** (§4): the Theorem 5.5 formula and the
  `extRS` ladder run in the same engine as the ladder, machine-checked equivalent to it
  wherever they complete, with the failure mode of each recorded. ATTACK-6 counts
  variables; this measures.
* **Exact coding dimensions over non-binary alphabets** (§5.4): 4 for the two generalized
  Thue-Morse words, 6 for `t × p`, **7 for `t × r`** — the last from a complete decision of
  all 176 words `v` with `Σv <= 8`, so exact rather than certified-from-below. Plus the
  observation that the nonempty `v` are not determined by `Σv` or by the letter multiset
  (`P_{211} ≠ ∅`, `P_{121} = ∅`; `P_{331} ≠ ∅`, `P_{313} = ∅`).
* **A third verification layer**: playing the Alice/Bob game from the definition
  (`brute_pv_game`), which checks Lemma 1 / the source's Prop. 5.3 itself, not just the
  ladder. ATTACK-6's two brute forces are both strategy-tree recursions.

**Failed / not done.**
* **Problem 10.2 as literally posed — general (non-uniform) substitutions — is not
  solved.** `docs/OPEN-TARGETS.md` #7 scopes the attack to `k`-uniform substitutions, and
  for that restriction the effectiveness half was already available from the source's own
  Theorem 5.5. The Dumont-Thomas question the authors raise for general `τ` is untouched.
* No closed form for `P_111` / `P_1111` of Rudin-Shapiro, nor for any `P_v` of the product
  words; those remain automata.
* `S` is not machine-proved above `B = 6-7` (the counting query costs `k^{B+1}` symbols and
  blows past 4 GB); the larger `S` values in §5.4 are prefix counts. Same limit ATTACK-6
  hit.
* The random-morphism sweep for high coding dimension (`explore/attack7_sweep.py`) was a
  dead end: the sequences with the largest right-special counts are exactly the ones whose
  `factorEq` automaton is large (710-950 states), and their ladders exceed 3-4 GB. The
  dimension-7 example came from a construction (letterwise products), not the sweep.
  ATTACK-6 got past this with hill-climbing plus a certificate route that never builds
  `FE`; that route was not re-implemented here.
* No general formula for `dim W(X × Y)` in terms of `dim W(X)`, `dim W(Y)`, though
  `3 + 4 = 7` and `3 + 3 = 6` are suggestive.
* The `S`-based bound is tight in 7 of 13 sequences and loose by up to 2 elsewhere; no
  sharper counting bound was found (ATTACK-6 §3.3 measures the same slack in the binary
  case).

**Machine-verified.** 34 closed sentences (`results/attack7_chars.json`, 0 failures); 100
`(sequence, v)` records agreeing across three independent computations
(`results/attack7_brute*.json`, 0 mismatches); 176 + 95 decided words `v` for the two
product sequences (`results/attack7_products.json`); the three-encoding equivalence and
cost table (`results/attack7_compare.json`); coding dimensions for 13 sequences
(`results/attack7_main.json`, `results/attack7_bound.json`).
