# ATTACK TARGET 2 — a tight family for the linear-subsequence state-complexity bound

**Refereed:** see `paper/attack2-verdict.md` — verdict **PROVED**.

**Verdict: solved.** The generalized Thue–Morse family `h_m(i) = s_2(i) mod m` answers
Open Problem 1 of Moradi–Rampersad–Shallit with `C = 1/2` for *every* odd `n >= 3`
(and with `C -> 1 - 1/(3m) >= 5/6` along `n = 3*2^(p-1)+1`).  The exact state count is
proved here, modulo two literature citations, and machine-verified three independent ways.
`C = 1/2` is **exactly attained** at `(m,n) = (2,3)` (`sc = 6 = (1/2)*3*4`), so `1/2` is the
largest constant valid for all odd `n >= 3` in this family, not merely a safe lower bound.

**On significance.** This is an assembly of three existing pieces, not a new hard theorem:
the paper's own Theorem 16 proof generalises verbatim to Lemma A; Lemma 15 needs a
replacement, supplied here by a two-line construction plus Gelfond's 1968 equidistribution
theorem (Lemma B); and the `m^2 n` growth rate is already in Tromp–Shallit 1995 / Starosta
2012. What was missing from the source was exactly the join of the three. That does not
make the answer less correct, but it should be read as a routine composition, not a
technically deep new result.

## 1. The problem, verbatim, and its status

Source: D. Moradi, N. Rampersad, J. Shallit, *Complexity of Linear Subsequences of
`k`-Automatic Sequences*, arXiv:2512.10017, **v5 (2 Apr 2026)**, §4, immediately after
Corollary 10:

> **Open Problem 1.** Find a class of automatic sequences `(h_m(i))_{i>=0}`, generated by a
> DFAO of `m` states with msd-first input, such that there is a constant `C` so the linear
> subsequences `(h_m(ni))_{i>=0}` have at least the state complexity `C n m^2` for
> infinitely many `n`.

Context in the source:

* **Theorem 9(a)** (their construction). For `c < n`, `(h(ni+c))` is generated by a DFAO
  whose states are the length-`n` factors of the *interior* sequence `h'` occurring at
  positions `n[x]`:  `q'(x) = [h'(n[x]), ..., h'(n[x]+n-1)]`, `tau'([r_0..r_{n-1}]) = tau(r_c)`.
  This construction — and hence Theorem C and every `#windows` count below — needs the
  DFAO to be **zero-consistent**, `delta(q_0,0) = q_0`; otherwise `h'(0) != q_0` and the
  induction `h'(ki+b) = delta(h'(i),b)` fails at `i = 0`. Both families used here
  (`t_{2,m}` and the base-3 family of §4) are zero-consistent.
* **Corollary 10(a).** Hence `sc(h(ni+c)) <= rho_{h'}(n) <= k n m^2` (the last step is
  Theorem 1 = [Allouche–Shallit, Thm 10.3.1], `rho_x(n) <= k n rho_{M̂}(2) <= k n m^2`).
* "We do not know if the bounds in Corollary 10 are tight."
* **Theorem 14** settles the case `m = 2`: `sc(t(ni)) = rho_t(n / nu_2(n))` for Thue–Morse,
  via **Lemma 15** (all length-`n` factors of `t` are reachable, for odd `n`, citing
  Blanchet-Sadri et al.) and **Theorem 16** (the Theorem-9 construction is optimal for
  odd `n`).  What is missing is the `m`-dependence: a family in which the `m^2` shows up.

Status re-verified 2026-08-17: v5 is the latest version; the follow-up paper
arXiv:2603.21645 (Moradi–Rampersad–Shallit, *Fibonacci-automatic* case, Mar 2026) restates
the base-`k` work as [12] and does **not** settle Open Problem 1 (its own open problems are
about `(t(i+c))` in the Fibonacci system and about the Tribonacci word).  Open Problem 1 is
also stated, verbatim, as **Problem 1** in Delaram Moradi's MMath thesis, *State Complexity
of Linear Relations and Linear Subsequences of Automatic Sequences* (University of Waterloo,
UWSpace `10012/23044`, deposited 2026-04-23); the thesis's own "More Open Problems" section
adds further problems and does not resolve this one either. So: open, as of the most recent
authoritative source (the thesis, April 2026), by the problem's own author.

## 2. How it is expressed here

`sc` = number of states of the minimal msd-first DFAO. Two independent computations:

* **Engine** (`explore/attack2_engine.py`). A DFAO given as a `k`-uniform morphism +
  coding, the subsequence as the term `n*i`:

      mode msd
      def T 2 3 0 01 12 20 012          # h_3(i) = s_2(i) mod 3
      dfa T[31*i]=0                     # -> DFA vars=[i] states=228 (msd base 2)

  No `learnfe`, no layered `let`, no escape hatch: `T[n*i]=a` is quantifier-free and the
  engine's own front end minimises it. Reading the per-letter `dfa T[n*i]=a` state count as
  the DFAO size **assumes** that for these families every level set `{i : h(ni)=a}` needs
  the full `sc` states; this is not proved, but it held in all 14 engine cells checked
  (every output letter gave the same state count, equal to `sc`), so the engine numbers
  below stand as an empirical bridge, not a proof.
* **Direct construction** (`explore/attack2_core.py`). Build Theorem 9(a)'s automaton
  (states = length-`n` windows as byte strings, BFS from `[h'(0..n-1)]`), then Moore-minimise.
  This gives exact `sc` for `m` up to 10 and `n` into the hundreds, far past what enumerating
  DFA states through the logic front end would be worth.

Both were checked against the paper's own closed form for Thue–Morse.

## 3. Result 1 — the answer to Open Problem 1

Let `t_{2,m} = (s_2(i) mod m)_{i>=0}` be the generalized Thue–Morse word, generated by the
`m`-state msd DFAO `delta(q,d) = q+d mod m`, identity coding (`def T 2 m 0 01 12 ... `).
It is minimal (all states reachable, all outputs distinct), and `h(2i) = h(i)`, so
`sc(h(ni))` depends only on the odd part of `n`.

> **Theorem A.** For every `m >= 2` and every odd `n >= 3`,
>
>       sc(t_{2,m}(ni)) = rho_{t_{2,m}}(n) = m^2 (n-1) - m * max(2^(p-1), r),
>       where n-1 = 2^p + r with 0 < r <= 2^p.
>
> Consequently, for all odd `n >= 3`
>
>       sc(t_{2,m}(ni)) >= (1 - 1/(2m)) m^2 (n-1) >= (3/4) m^2 (n-1) >= (1/2) n m^2,
>
> and along `n = 3*2^(p-1)+1` (`p >= 2`) the value is exactly `(1 - 1/(3m)) m^2 (n-1)`, so
> `limsup_n sc(t_{2,m}(ni)) / (n m^2) = 1 - 1/(3m) >= 5/6`.
>
> **This answers Open Problem 1 with `C = 1/2`** — in fact for *all* odd `n`, not merely
> infinitely many, and with `C` arbitrarily close to `5/6` along a subsequence.

Relative to the bound actually proved in the paper (`k n m^2 = 2 n m^2`), the family
realises a fraction `(1-1/(3m))/2`, i.e. `0.417 .. 0.5`: **Corollary 10 is tight in the
shape `n m^2`, and loose only in the constant `k`** (see §5).

### 3.1 Proof

Write `s = s_2`, `h = t_{2,m}`, and note `h` is *digit-additive*:
`h(k^L X + y) = h(X) + h(y) (mod m)` for `0 <= y < k^L` (here `k = 2`), because the base-`k`
representation of `k^L X + y` is that of `X` followed by the length-`L` representation of `y`.

**Lemma A (Nerode classes are exactly the windows).** Let `h` be any `k`-digit-additive
sequence mod `m` and `n >= 1`. For a word `x` over `Sigma_k` put
`W(x) = (h(n[x]+g) mod m)_{0<=g<n}`. Then two inputs `x, y` are Myhill–Nerode equivalent for
the sequence `(h(ni))_{i>=0}` **iff** `W(x) = W(y)`.  Hence

      sc(h(ni)) = #{ (h(nj+g) mod m)_{0<=g<n} : j >= 0 }.

*Proof.* For `z` of length `L`: `n[xz] = k^L n[x] + n[z]`. Write `n[z] = k^L g + w` with
`0 <= w < k^L`; then `g = floor(n[z]/k^L) <= n-1`, and `n[xz] = k^L(n[x]+g) + w`, so by
digit-additivity `h(n[xz]) = h(n[x]+g) + h(w)`. If `W(x) = W(y)` the two outputs agree for
every `z`, so `x ~ y`. Conversely suppose `W(x)[g] != W(y)[g]`. Choose `L` with `k^L >= n`.
As `Z` runs through `0,...,k^L-1`, `floor(nZ/k^L)` starts at `0`, ends at `n-1` and increases
by at most `1` per step (because `n <= k^L`), so it takes the value `g` at some `Z`; let `z`
be the length-`L` representation of `Z`. Then `h(n[xz]) - h(n[yz]) = W(x)[g] - W(y)[g] != 0`,
so `x !~ y`. ∎

Lemma A generalises the paper's Theorem 16 (which is the case `k=2, m=2`, odd `n`) to every
digit-additive sequence and *every* `n`: for these sequences the Theorem-9(a) construction is
always optimal, no reachability lemma needed.

**Lemma B (every factor occurs at a multiple of `n`).** Let `m >= 2`, `h = t_{2,m}`, `n` odd.
Then every length-`n` factor of `h` occurs at some position divisible by `n`; hence, with
Lemma A, `sc(h(ni)) = rho_h(n)`.

*Proof.* Fix `t` with `2^t >= n` and write a position `P = 2^t a + b`, `0 <= b < 2^t`. For
`0 <= g < n <= 2^t`, either `b+g < 2^t` and `h(P+g) = h(a) + h(b+g)`, or `b+g >= 2^t` and
`h(P+g) = h(a+1) + h(b+g-2^t)`. So the factor at `P` is determined by the *type*
`(u,v,b) = (h(a), h(a+1), b)`.

Given `P`, we produce `j` with `nj` of the same type. As `n` is odd it is invertible mod
`2^t`; pick `j_0 in [0,2^t)` with `n j_0 = b (mod 2^t)`. For `j = j_0 + 2^t j'` we get
`nj = 2^t (a_0 + n j') + b` with `a_0 = (n j_0 - b)/2^t >= 0`, so the high part runs through
the arithmetic progression `a_0 + n j'`.

Choose `T in {1,...,m}` with `T = u - v + 1 (mod m)`, choose any `L > T`, and look for
`a' = 2^L A + (2^T - 1)`. Bit `T` of `a'` is `0`, so `s(a') = s(A) + T`, and
`a'+1 = 2^L A + 2^T`, so `s(a'+1) = s(A) + 1`. Hence `(h(a'), h(a'+1)) = (u,v)` as soon as
`h(A) = v-1 (mod m)`. The requirement `a' = a_0 (mod n)` is `2^L A = a_0 - 2^T + 1 (mod n)`,
i.e. a single congruence `A = A_0 (mod n)` since `2` is invertible mod the odd `n`. By
**Gelfond's theorem** (A. O. Gelfond, *Sur les nombres qui ont des propriétés additives et
multiplicatives données*, Acta Arith. 13 (1968) 259–265: for base `q` and `gcd(m, q-1) = 1`
— automatic for `q = 2` — `s_q` is equidistributed mod `m` in every arithmetic progression)
there are infinitely many such `A` with `s(A) = v-1 (mod m)`. Any large one gives
`j' = (a'-a_0)/n >= 0`, hence the required `j`. ∎

Lemma B is the `m`-general replacement for the paper's Lemma 15 (which quoted
Blanchet-Sadri et al. for Thue–Morse only); it also re-proves their Theorem 14 for `m = 2`
without that citation.

**Known input: the factor complexity of `t_{2,m}`.** `rho_{t_{2,m}}` was determined by
J. Tromp and J. Shallit, *Subword complexity of a generalized Thue–Morse word*, Inform.
Process. Lett. **54** (1995) 313–316 (exactly `y(i) = s_2(i) mod k`), and again, for all bases,
by Š. Starosta, *Generalized Thue–Morse words and palindromic richness*, Kybernetika **48**
(2012) 361–370, Table 2: with `q` = order of `b-1` in `Z_m` (`q = m` for `b = 2`),

      C(n) = q m (n-1) - m (b^k - b^{k-1})       for n = b^k + 1 + l,  0 <= l < b^k - b^{k-1}
      C(n) = q m (n-1) - m (b^k - b^{k-1} + l)   for n = (2b-1) b^{k-1} + 1 + l, 0 <= l < b^(k+1) - 2 b^k + b^(k-1)

For `b = 2` this is exactly `m^2(n-1) - m * max(2^(p-1), r)` with `n-1 = 2^p + r`,
`0 < r <= 2^p` (and it specialises at `m = 2` to Brlek's Thue–Morse formula, which is how the
source paper's Corollary 17 is derived). Theorem A follows from Lemma A + Lemma B + this
formula. The bounds quoted in Theorem A use `max(2^(p-1), r) <= (n-1)/2` (both `2^(p-1)` and
`r` are at most `(n-1)/2`, with equality at `r = 2^p`, i.e. `n-1 = 2^(p+1)`), and
`max = (n-1)/3` at `n-1 = 3*2^(p-1)`. ∎

**Edge case at `p = 0`.** For `n = 3`, `n-1 = 1 = 2^0 + 0`, forcing `p = 0` and making
`2^(p-1) = 1/2` a half-integer; the formula is still correct only because
`max(1/2, r) = max(1/2, 1) = 1` picks out `r`. Read `2^(p-1)` as a real number here (or
treat `n = 3` as a base case), not as requiring `p >= 1`. The value itself is right
(checked `m = 2..20`, `n = 3`).

## 4. Result 2 — the empirically extremal family (base 3)

Sweeping *all* `m`-state DFAOs (`explore/attack2_sweep.py`; the coding may be taken to be
the identity, since the intrinsic automaton of a minimal `m`-state DFAO is itself an
`m`-state DFAO and coarsening a coding cannot increase `sc`), the maximiser at `k = 3` is

      h_m(i) = (number of digits equal to 2 in the base-3 representation of i) mod m
      def T 3 m 0 001 112 223 ... (identity coding)

with, machine-verified for `m = 2..8` and 33 values of `n <= 244`,

> **Observation B.**  `sc(h_m(ni)) = m^2 (n-1)` **exactly**, for every `n` with `3 ∤ n`
> (and `sc(h_m(ni)) = sc(h_m(n' i))` for `n = 3^v n'`).  Its factor complexity is the same
> number: `rho_{h_m}(n) = m^2(n-1)`.

Lemma A applies verbatim (the sequence is 3-digit-additive with weights `(0,0,1)`), so
`sc = #{windows at multiples of n}`; the two missing ingredients relative to Theorem A are
the analogue of Lemma B (Gelfond's theorem covers `s_q` mod `m` with `gcd(m,q-1)=1`, which
for base 3 and even `m` fails, though the weighted digit sum here is not `s_3`) and a proof
of `rho = m^2(n-1)`. Both are **conjectural** here, verified only by machine. This family
reaches `sc/(n m^2) = 1 - 1/n`, i.e. it saturates `n m^2`, but only `1/3` of the paper's
`k n m^2`.

## 5. Result 3 — Corollary 10 is loose in `k`, tight in `n m^2`

> **Theorem C (sharpening of Corollary 10(a)).** Let `h` be generated by an `m`-state
> **zero-consistent** (`delta(q_0,0) = q_0`) msd-first DFAO with interior sequence `h'`,
> let `n >= 1` and let `t` be minimal with `k^t >= n`. Then
>
>       rho_{h'}(n) <= rho_{M̂}(2) * (n-1) + m * (k^t - n + 1) <= m^2 (n-1) + m ((k-1) n + 1),
>
> and hence `sc(h(ni+c)) <= m^2(n-1) + m((k-1)n + 1)` for `c < n`.

*Proof.* Write a position `P = k^t a + b`, `0 <= b < k^t`. Every position in `[P, P+n)` is
`k^t(a+e) + r` with `e in {0,1}` and `0 <= r < k^t`, and `h'(k^t A + r) = delta(h'(A), (r)_k)`
because msd reading concatenates digit blocks. So the length-`n` factor of `h'` at `P` is
determined by `(h'(a), h'(a+1), b)`, and by `(h'(a), b)` alone when `b + n <= k^t`. There are
`k^t - n + 1` values of `b` of the latter kind and `n-1` of the former, giving the count. The
final bound uses `rho_{M̂}(2) <= m^2` and `k^t < k n`. ∎

The paper's chain gives `k n m^2`; Theorem C gives `n m^2 (1 + (k-1)/m) + O(m)`, i.e. it
removes the factor `k` whenever `m >> k`. Sweep data (best `sc` over weighted-digit-sum
DFAOs and 2000–4000 random DFAOs per cell, `n = 31`):

     k  m   best sc   /(n m^2)   /(k n m^2)   maximiser
     2  2       92      0.742      0.371      digit sum mod 2 (Thue–Morse)
     2  3      228      0.817      0.409      digit sum mod 3
     2  4      424      0.855      0.427      digit sum mod 4
     2  5      680      0.877      0.439      digit sum mod 5
     3  2      120      0.968      0.323      #2s mod 2
     3  5      750      0.968      0.323      #2s mod 5
     4  3      312      1.118      0.280      weights (0,0,1,0)
     4  5      820      1.058      0.265      weights (0,0,1,3)
     5  3      300      1.075      0.215      weights (0,0,1,2,1)
     5  5      800      1.032      0.206      weights (0,0,1,0,2)

Two things to note. (i) **A group automaton always attains the maximum** (a weighted digit
sum `q -> q + w_d mod m`) — but it is not always the *unique* maximiser: exhausting all
zero-consistent identity-coded 3-state base-3 DFAOs at `n = 7` finds the maximum `54 =
m^2(n-1)` attained by **42** automata, of which only **18** are group automata; e.g.
`delta = [[0,0,1],[1,0,2],[2,2,0]]` attains 54 and is not a group automaton (`delta(.,1)`
is not a permutation). So "no random DFAO ever won" is too strong — the defensible claim
is that a group automaton is always *among* the maximisers. (ii) `sc <= m^2(n-1)` holds in all
of `k = 2, 3` (exhaustively over *all* 2- and 3-state DFAOs at `n = 7,11,16,31`) but is
**false** for `k = 4`: the group automaton with weights `(0,0,1,0)`, i.e.
`h(i) = (number of digits equal to 2 in (i)_4) mod 3`, has `sc(h(31 i)) = 312 > 270 =
m^2(n-1)` (still under Theorem C's bound of `372`); a random 3-state 4-DFAO,
`delta = [[0,2,0,0],[1,1,0,1],[2,1,2,2]]`, reaches the same 312. So the truth for the
maximum over `m`-state `k`-DFAOs sits between `m^2(n-1)` and `m^2(n-1) + m((k-1)n+1)`; the
`k` in `k n m^2` is a real, but bounded, effect and vanishes as `m` grows.

## 6. Ledger

**Known before (cited, not claimed):**
* Theorem 9 / Corollary 10 / Theorem 14 / Theorem 16 / Lemma 15 of arXiv:2512.10017.
* `rho_x(n) <= k n rho_{M̂}(2) <= k n m^2` [Allouche–Shallit Thm 10.3.1].
* `rho_{t_{2,m}}(n)` exact formula [Tromp–Shallit 1995; Starosta 2012, Table 2]; Brlek's
  Thue–Morse case.
* Gelfond's equidistribution theorem for `s_q mod m` in arithmetic progressions (1968).

**New here:**
* **Lemma A** — for any `k`-digit-additive sequence and *any* `n`, the Nerode classes of
  `(h(ni))` are exactly the length-`n` windows of `h` at multiples of `n`; the Theorem-9(a)
  construction is optimal. (Generalises their Theorem 16 from `k=m=2`, odd `n`.) *Proved.*
* **Lemma B** — for odd `n`, every length-`n` factor of `t_{2,m}` occurs at a multiple of
  `n`. (Generalises their Lemma 15 from `m=2`; replaces the Blanchet-Sadri citation with
  Gelfond.) *Proved.*
* **Theorem A** — exact `sc(t_{2,m}(ni))` for all odd `n`, hence **an answer to Open
  Problem 1** with `C = 1/2` for all odd `n` and `C -> 1-1/(3m) >= 5/6` along a subsequence.
  *Proved* (given the two citations above), and machine-verified on 945 + 125 cells.
* **Theorem C** — `rho_{h'}(n) <= rho_{M̂}(2)(n-1) + m(k^t-n+1)`, removing the factor `k`
  from Corollary 10 for `m >> k`. *Proved.*
* **Observation B** — the base-3 `#2s mod m` family with `sc = m^2(n-1)` exactly, the
  empirical maximiser; and the sweep finding that *a* group automaton always attains the
  maximum (not always uniquely — 42 maximisers, 18 of them group automata, at `k=3,m=3,
  n=7`). *Machine-verified, not proved.*

**Not established / open:**
* The `rho = m^2(n-1)` formula and the Lemma-B analogue for the base-3 family (Observation B).
* The exact maximum of `sc(h(ni))` over `m`-state `k`-DFAOs for `k >= 4` (between
  `m^2(n-1)` and `m^2(n-1)+m((k-1)n+1)`).
* Whether the `c > 0` case (`sc(h(ni+c))`, and the paper's Corollary 10(b)) behaves the same;
  only `c = 0` was measured here, since that is what Open Problem 1 asks.

**Failed / discarded on the way:**
* The first plan — an lsd/msd ladder with `learnfe` and layered `let`-predicates — was
  unnecessary: `dfa T[n*i]=a` compiles directly and the Theorem-9(a) construction is faster
  still. No budget escalation beyond 4 GB / 900 s was ever needed (largest engine run: 0.1 s).
* Conjecture `sc <= m^2(n-1)` for all `k`: refuted at `k = 4` (§5).
* Random-DFAO search as a route to the extremal family: never beat the group automata; the
  hunt only became productive after the `(h'(a), h'(a+1), b)` parametrisation of §5 said
  where to look (group automata make the pair `(h'(a), h'(a+1))` take all `m^2` values).
